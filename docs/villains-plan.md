# The Villain's Plan (Mythic Magazine Vol. 69, pp. 16–28)

A Mythic GME tool for revealing a villain's scheme in stages during solo/GM-less play.
Related: "The Villain Crafter" (Mythic Magazine #41) builds the villain, organization,
lieutenants, and minions.

## Purpose

Turns "we don't know what the villain wants" into an investigative arc that is revealed
one layer at a time, instead of a single "Aha!" jump. Guarantees three narrative beats:

1. Piecewise reveal of the plan (Discovery phases).
2. A definite moment where the whole scheme snaps into focus (End Goal).
3. Optional post-defeat escalation (Pivot Plan / Plan B).

## Prerequisite

The PC must already be aware of a villain and that some plan is in progress
(e.g. hired to investigate a reactivated temple, or a general who has gone rogue).

## Arc structure

| Arc | Content | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discovery | Scenes earning and generating plan phases, ending with the End Goal | Longest |
| 2. Foiling | Scenes working to stop the plan | Equal to or longer than Arc 1 |
| 3. Pivot (optional) | Villain's desperate Plan B | 1–3 Scenes only |

## Earned Discovery (no dice for *when*)

Play normally with Mythic. When you judge that the PC has *done enough* — survived
perils, followed leads, infiltrated somewhere, reached a scene where information would
naturally surface — you grant a Discovery. Deliberately **not** a Fate Question: since
you already decided the mystery is the adventure's spine, momentum decides, not dice.
"You'll know it when you see it."

## Generating a phase

1. **End Goal Roll first** (see below) to check whether this Discovery is the finale.
2. Roll `d100` on **Villain Plan Focus** for Context.
3. Roll two Keywords on **Plot Twists**.
4. Interpret Focus + Keywords against current adventure Context; weave into the
   existing narrative so it all coheres.

Each phase must **explain something and open new questions** — the new questions are
the PC's next leads (a seized mine, a hired mercenary, a named but unknown enemy).

## End Goal Roll

Before generating each new phase: roll `d10`, **+2 per phase already discovered**.

- Total **11+** → this Discovery is the End Goal (roll on End Goal Focus instead).
- Total **10 or less** → another ordinary phase; overall plan still unknown.

So: 1st Discovery +0, 2nd +2, 3rd +4, 4th +6, 5th +8 (guaranteed at 5th; possible from
the 2nd onward on a 9–10).

The End Goal must not only give the final phase/goal but **retroactively explain and
tie together every earlier phase** into a coherent whole.

## Pivot Plan (Plan B)

Available when the villain survives their plan's defeat, key underlings are at large,
or a pre-arranged failsafe is plausible. Roll Pivot Plan Focus + two Plot Twist
Keywords. It should be desperate, a grab at partial victory, or petty revenge —
short, and never overshadowing the main plan.

Two ways to run it:

- **Decided**: choose it during Bookkeeping of the Scene where the plan is defeated.
  Set up the next Expected Scene as a wind-down (celebration, loose ends) and reveal
  the Pivot there. Do not delay it — a known-but-pending pivot makes play feel padded.
- **Surprise**: finish the adventure, add one cleanup Scene, and ask the Fate Question
  "Does the villain enact a Pivot Plan?" (Yes / Exceptional Yes → yes).

## Tables

### Villain Plan Focus (d100) — Context of the plan's phase

| d100 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1–16 | **ON THE MARCH** — villain and/or henchmen/armies actively mobilizing for what looks like an ambitious offensive. |
| 17–24 | **RELOCATING** — villain is leaving their normal location/position for another place, for some reason. |
| 25–40 | **SEARCHING** — seeking something or someone, going to great lengths to find it. |
| 41–52 | **GATHERING RESOURCES** — collecting weapons, allies, equipment; massing strength for something big. |
| 53–60 | **TEAMWORK** — courting or working with someone toward a mutual goal: a peer, an enemy, or a specialist. |
| 61–72 | **COLLECTING** — specific objects or people that don't obviously go together or hold intrinsic value, but must matter to the plan. |
| 73–80 | **CONSTRUCTION** — building something (building, excavation, vehicle) for an unknown reason. |
| 81–100 | **NO CONTEXT** — no added Context; interpret from Keywords alone. |

### End Goal Focus (d100) — the plan's final phase

| d100 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1–18 | **ACQUISITION** — ambitious acquisition: conquer a nation, control a corporation. |
| 19–36 | **PERSONAL** — rooted in something deeply personal: revenge, raising the dead, righting a past wrong. |
| 37–50 | **ADVANCEMENT** — self-elevation: sole heir to the throne, ascending to godhood, enhanced power. |
| 51–61 | **DESTRUCTION** — destroy a world, installation, or organization; the villain has a reason why. |
| 62–72 | **A NEW ORDER** — sees the world as broken and fixes it monstrously: end crime via thought surveillance, end a conflict by obliterating a country. |
| 73–76 | **VILLAIN BEHIND THE VILLAIN** — this villain is the public face of the real one controlling them; interpret who and why. |
| 77–83 | **SURVIVAL** — the scheme exists to save the villain, possibly extremely: immortality at mass cost, hoarding food neighbors need. |
| 84–100 | **NO CONTEXT** — interpret the End Goal from Keywords alone. |

### Pivot Plan Focus (d100) — what does the villain do?

| d100 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1–40 | **THEY DO WHAT THEY CAN** — a much diminished version of the original plan (sack one town instead of conquering the nation). |
| 41–65 | **IF I CAN'T HAVE IT NO ONE CAN** — ruin it for everyone (failed to steal the item → destroy the item). |
| 66–95 | **REVENGE** — petty revenge, striking at those who stopped them, even for no gain. |
| 96–100 | **SURPRISE MOVE** — something unexpected, possibly a wholly different goal; interpret from Plot Twist Keywords. |

## Worked examples from the article

### General Gorazon (fantasy)

Setup: a victorious Uhurakan general stays encamped at the defeated sorcerer's
stronghold, absorbing the mage's henchmen. PC investigates.

| Discovery | Roll | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Gathering Resources + Mental, Missing | Agents at a burned-out barn report amassing artifacts, mercenaries, and a seized ore mine — but they are *missing the knowledge* of why. |
| 2 | — | Operative sent into a dungeon for a powerful relic. |
| 3 | — | Scattered forces organized into an army. |
| 4 | d10 5 + 6 = 11 → End Goal: Personal + Limit, Power | The king, not the sorcerer, started the war out of lust for dark power. Gorazon means to *limit* the king's *power* permanently — via his own ruinous war; the ore enhances the dark artifacts to offset the king's military strength. |

Foiling: PC blows the mine, destroying the ore and most artifacts; Gorazon is left
vulnerable but escapes.

Pivot: Revenge + Diminish, Mystery → Gorazon raids the PC's storehouse, kills the
guards, steals two artifacts of unknown function; the king arrives soon, so the PC
must hunt him down immediately.

### Cold Rock, Cold Heart (spy)

PC Philip Larkin vs. billionaire Max Vathen and his private island nation.

| Discovery | Roll | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Construction + Rare, Enemy | Infiltrating the investors' party: a drunk investor lets slip Vathen is *building* something on the island to destroy his most hated enemies — "something the world has never seen". |
| 2 | d10 4 + 2 = 6 → Gathering Resources + Problem, Failure | A miles-wide mine for *wazonite*, unique to the island; notes on "heat transfer" and "fusion capable" — solving the *problem* of sustained fusion. |
| 3 | d10 8 + 4 = 12 → End Goal: Destruction + Personal, Unknown | Wazonite absorbs near-infinite heat without heating; fusion is only the charging tool. A thimble becomes an untraceable heat bomb; Vathen will plant them among rivals worldwide and kill them all at once, undetectable, leaving him master of his world atop a secret stockpile. |

Foiling: Larkin is discovered, flees into the freezing wazonite-laced core, sets a
charge on processed ore, escapes by helicopter as the island destroys itself.

Pivot: Revenge + Incapacitate, Repair → Vathen, disguised as an AC *repair* technician
during a hotel outage, tries to *incapacitate* Larkin with a tranquilizer dart and
leave him with a wazonite ampule. Larkin disarms him and tranquilizes him instead.

## Design notes to keep in mind when using this

- Discovery timing is a judgment call, never a Fate Question. Dice only shape *what* is
  learned and *whether* it is the End Goal.
- Every non-final phase should leave the PC with a concrete next lead.
- The End Goal is a retro-explanation obligation: if it doesn't make the earlier phases
  make sense, reinterpret until it does.
- The Pivot arc is a coda, not a second adventure; cap it at a Scene or three.
